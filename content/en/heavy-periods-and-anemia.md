[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Heavy periods can, over time, take out more iron than your body replaces
from food — since iron is needed to make haemoglobin (the part of blood that carries oxygen), this can
lead to iron-deficiency anaemia. This is a common, recognised pattern, not something unusual to you.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Signs of anaemia include ongoing tiredness, weakness, feeling short of
breath, looking pale, or having cold hands and feet. These can be easy to dismiss as "just tired,"
especially if you've gotten used to heavy periods — but they're worth mentioning together to a
clinician, who can check with a simple blood test.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Heavy bleeding itself can have several possible causes, and anaemia is
usually treated by addressing both the bleeding and the iron level — this app doesn't recommend
supplements or doses, but a clinician can advise on what's right for you. See "Heavy bleeding" for signs
that your flow may be heavier than typical.
