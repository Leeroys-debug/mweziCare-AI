[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Many people notice physical and emotional changes in the days before their
period — mood swings, irritability, feeling low or anxious, bloating, breast tenderness, and tiredness
are all common and are usually called premenstrual syndrome (PMS). These symptoms typically ease once
your period starts.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] For a smaller number of people, premenstrual mood changes are much more
severe — intense mood swings, anger, anxiety, or low mood that seriously disrupts school, work, or
relationships every cycle. This more severe pattern is sometimes called PMDD, and it's a recognised
condition a clinician can help with, not something to just push through.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] If premenstrual symptoms ever include thoughts of harming yourself, treat
that as urgent and reach out for support right away rather than waiting for your period to start.
Tracking how you feel across your cycle, not just physical symptoms, can help you and a clinician spot
a pattern worth discussing.
