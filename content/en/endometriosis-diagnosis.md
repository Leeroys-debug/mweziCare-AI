[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] There's no simple blood test or scan that can rule endometriosis in or out
on its own. An ultrasound or MRI can sometimes spot larger areas of endometriosis, but normal scan
results don't mean it isn't there. The most definite way to diagnose it is a small operation called a
laparoscopy, where a clinician looks inside the pelvis with a camera and can take a tissue sample to
confirm it.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Because of this, getting a diagnosis often takes a long time — research has
found average delays of several years between symptoms starting and a diagnosis being made, partly
because the main test is surgical and partly because symptoms are sometimes dismissed as "just bad
period pain." This delay is a recognised problem, not a reflection of how serious or real your pain is.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] A clinician will usually start by asking about your symptoms and cycle
history — a tracked log or clinician summary from this app can help with that conversation — before
deciding whether imaging or referral to a specialist is the next step.
