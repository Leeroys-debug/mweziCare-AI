A first period usually arrives somewhere between about ages 10 and 15, though a bit earlier or later
is still normal. It's common to notice signs beforehand too — things like white vaginal discharge in
the months leading up to it.

Your first period is often lighter and shorter than the ones that follow, and the blood can look
brown, pink, or red rather than always bright red — all of that is normal. Cycles are often irregular
for the first year or two as your body settles into a pattern, so don't worry if your next period
doesn't arrive exactly on the same schedule.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] If you haven't had a period by age 15, or haven't shown other signs of
puberty by 13, it's worth seeing a clinician. Otherwise, this app's Track tab is a good place to start
getting familiar with your own cycle from the very first period onward.
