Understanding a few body parts makes it easier to follow what happens during your cycle.

The ovaries are two small organs that store eggs and produce hormones like oestrogen and progesterone,
which drive your cycle. Each cycle, one ovary usually releases a mature egg — this is ovulation. The
fallopian tubes connect each ovary to the uterus and are where the egg travels, and where fertilisation
happens if sperm meets the egg there. The uterus (womb) is a muscular organ where a fertilised egg
would implant and a pregnancy would grow; its inner lining is what builds up and sheds as your period
if pregnancy doesn't happen. The cervix is the narrow lower part of the uterus, opening into the
vagina — the canal connecting the cervix to the outside of the body — which is also where menstrual
blood, and, during childbirth, a baby, pass through.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] This is general anatomy, not specific to any individual — bodies vary, and
some people are born with differences in these organs. If you have questions about your own anatomy, a
clinician is the right person to ask.
