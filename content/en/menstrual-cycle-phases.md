Your menstrual cycle has four phases, driven by changing hormone levels, that repeat roughly every
21 to 35 days.

The menstrual phase is your period itself — the lining of the womb sheds because hormone levels are at
their lowest. The follicular phase overlaps with this and continues afterward, as rising oestrogen
helps an egg mature in the ovary. Around the midpoint of the cycle, a surge in another hormone (LH)
triggers ovulation — the release of that egg. In the luteal phase that follows, progesterone rises to
prepare the womb lining; if pregnancy doesn't occur, hormone levels drop and the cycle starts again
with a new period.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Cycle length and how each phase feels can vary a lot between people, and
even from cycle to cycle for the same person — tracking over time is more useful than comparing to a
fixed "textbook" cycle.
