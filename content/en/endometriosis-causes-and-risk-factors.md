[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Doctors don't know exactly what causes endometriosis. The most studied
explanation is that menstrual blood containing tissue similar to the womb lining can flow backward
into the pelvis instead of leaving the body, where it can implant and grow. Other theories include
hormone-driven changes in cells, tissue transported through blood or lymph, and an immune system that
doesn't clear this tissue the way it normally would.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Some things are linked to a higher chance of developing endometriosis,
though having one or more of them doesn't mean someone will get it: starting periods at a young age,
short menstrual cycles, heavy or long periods, never having given birth, and having a close relative
(such as a mother or sister) with endometriosis.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] None of this is caused by anything a person did or didn't do — endometriosis
isn't the result of a lifestyle choice. This information is for understanding, not for working out
whether you have it yourself; only a clinician can assess that.
