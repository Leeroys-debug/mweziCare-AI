[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Menstruation — a period — is the normal monthly shedding of the lining of
the uterus (womb), which leaves the body through the vagina as blood and tissue. It happens because,
each cycle, hormones from the ovaries and brain prepare the uterus lining to support a possible
pregnancy; if no pregnancy happens, that lining isn't needed, so the body sheds it and starts building
a new one for the next cycle.

[CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED] Menstruation usually starts at puberty — commonly somewhere between ages 10
and 15 — and continues roughly monthly until menopause, decades later, with pauses during pregnancy and
sometimes while breastfeeding. A period itself typically lasts around 2 to 7 days, though this varies
from person to person.

Menstruation is a normal, healthy part of the reproductive cycle, not an illness or something to feel
ashamed of. Understanding your own cycle — what's typical for you — is the foundation for everything
else this app helps with.
